YunoBall — Design Brief#

The design language: principles, tokens, and component rules. Runtime source of truth: apps/web/app/globals.css (tokens) and apps/web/app/lib/teamTheme.ts (team theming).

1. Personality & principles#

Quiet, dense, authoritative. A dark, StatMuse-inspired stats surface that feels like a professional tool, not a fan site — until a team enters the frame, at which point the interface takes on that team's colors.

  1. Content over chrome. Hierarchy comes from spacing, background contrast, and typography — not borders. Borders are rare by design (--border is a 5% white whisper).
  2. Neutral dominates; accent is earned. The lime brand accent appears only where it means something: search, selection, CTAs, links, positive metrics. Everything else stays neutral.
  3. Team color is context, not decoration. When the active context is one team (team page, player profile, single-team answer), the accent family re-tints to that team's branding. Backgrounds and neutrals never recolor.
  4. Dense but legible. Stat tables are the product. Small type sizes (a 12–17px text ramp) with strict contrast floors, not big type with thin data.
  5. Honest states. Skeletons while loading, plain-language refusals, coverage stated beside every mined claim. The UI never pretends to know something.

Ink#

--text #f5f5f5 · --muted #f2f2f4 · --faint #e7e7ec. The ramp is deliberately near-white top to bottom: hierarchy is carried by weight and a slight step-down, not by dimming secondary text to grey — which also means every tier clears 4.5:1 on every surface including the hover tier.

Brand accent (lime, used sparingly)#

--accent #89f336 with a full derived family: --accent-hover #9ff955, --accent-soft (16% fill), --accent-contrast #101c03 (near-black ink on solid accent), --accent-ink #9df84f (accent-colored text on dark needs the lighter tone; solid accent fills use --accent itself), --accent-border (42%). --accent-deep aliases --accent at brand level and exists so team themes have a handle to override. --chart mirrors --accent.

Semantic#

--success #4ade80 (+ --success-soft) · --danger #f87171 (+ --danger-soft, --danger-text #fca5a5 for ink on the soft fill). There is no warning tier.

3. Team theming (teamTheme.ts)#

The signature move. teamTheme(teamId) returns CSS-variable overrides scoped to a container — spreading it re-tints only the accent family to the team's primary color:

  • --accent, --accent-deep and --chart ← the team primary, unmodified — no lightening, darkening or muting. The primary drives solid surfaces.
  • --accent-hover, --accent-ink and --chart-strong ← the team's brand ink: readableInk() picks the most vivid palette color (chroma ≥ 0.25) that clears 3.4:1 on the dark canvas, else the first that clears 3.2:1, else the primary. Picking a real palette color rather than a lightened primary is what makes the theme show Seahawks green and Commanders gold instead of a washed-out tint.
  • --accent-soft → var(--elevated) and --accent-border → var(--border): deliberately not tinted. A low-opacity team color over the dark canvas goes muddy, so soft surfaces stay a clean neutral and the brand shows in the text and the solid fills.
  • --accent-contrast (ink on solid team surfaces) is contrast-checked: the first of the team's secondary then accent color that is near-neutral (chroma ≤ 0.2) and clears WCAG 4.5:1 against the primary; otherwise fall back to near-black #0c0d0f or white, whichever the primary carries. Strong second hues (navy + gold) are excluded on purpose — colored ink on a colored card reads badly — so player cards render as solid team color with guaranteed-readable ink.
  • All 32 team palettes (primary/secondary/accent) live in NFL_TEAM_COLORS, which also backs NFL_TEAM_NAMES so names and colors can't drift apart.

Rules: only accent-driven UI changes (buttons, active tabs, links, chart marks, stat highlights). Never re-tint backgrounds, neutrals, or semantic colors. Unknown team → teamTheme() returns undefined, no override (the lime brand accent stays).

4. Typography#

  • One face: Geist Variable (one bundled woff2 at public/fonts/Geist-Variable.woff2, weights 100–900, font-display: swap, no runtime font request). System-stack fallback. --display aliases --font — the same face carries everything from navigation copy through display metrics.
  • Text scale: caption 12 / small 13 / body 14 / ui 15 / body-lg 16 / lead 17, plus --fs-micro 10 for status glyphs and sort arrows.
  • Heading tiers, consolidated from the old 18–32px strays: h5 18 / h4 20 / h3 22 / h2 clamp(24px, 4.2vw, 28px) / h1 clamp(26px, 5vw, 32px) / --fs-hero clamp(38px, 4.6vw, 48px). A few page-defining headlines (the answer line, entity titles, focused result heads) still clamp per-context.
  • Numbers-first design: stat cells carry font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums so columns don't jitter between rows; narration reads at body sizes.

5. Space, shape, elevation, motion#

  • Spacing: 4px base scale, --s-1: 4px … --s-10: 40px — steps 1–6, then 8 and 10. The scale stops at 40px; larger gaps are page-level padding, set per-layout.
  • Radii: --r-sm 6 / --r-md 8 / --r-lg 12 / --r-xl 14 / --r-2xl 16 / --r-pill 999px. There is no single default: shared surfaces take --r-lg, cards --r-xl, and --r-2xl is reserved for the hero search family (input + suggest popover) so nothing else drifts that large.
  • Elevation: flat by philosophy — two shadows only, --shadow-card (a 1px hairline lift) and --shadow-pop (two-layer, for the mobile drawer and popovers); borders and background steps do the rest.
  • Z-scale: one documented ladder — sticky(1) < nav(10) < popover(40) < skip-link(100). Overlays render in-flow; no portals — each popover's parent stacks above table headers. On mobile the nav outranks --z-pop so its drawer and scrim clear any open dropdown behind them.
  • Motion: --ease: cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.8, 0.2, 1); --dur 160ms base and --dur-slow 240ms slow — no separate fast tier. Comparison bars animate their width at --dur; the shimmer sweep runs 1.3s. Motion supports comprehension, never decoration, and prefers-reduced-motion collapses all of it (infinite loops stop rather than spinning at 0.001ms).

7. Data-visualization rules#

  • The mirrored-bar comparison chart is currently the only chart. The leading bar takes --accent (team-tinted in team context), the trailing bar --border-strong; widths animate at --dur.
  • --chart / --chart-strong are declared and team-tinted but nothing consumes them yet — they are the reserved handle for real chart marks, so future viz doesn't reach for raw --accent.
  • Tables are the primary viz: every bar has its number beside it (each row is value | bar | label | bar | value). The chart sizes off a container query, not the viewport, so it renders the same in the main column and on the share page; under 560px the label takes its own line and the bars keep their length.

8. Accessibility bars#

  • Text contrast ≥ 4.5:1 on every surface tier it can sit on. The near-white ink ramp is what buys this: because no tier dims to grey, --faint clears the bar even on --bg-hover, the brightest surface.
  • Team-tinted ink is programmatically contrast-checked (WCAG relative luminance) before use; falls back to black/white.
  • Links inside running text are underlined — color alone is not a cue (WCAG 1.4.1). Standalone links (nav, cards, rows) identify themselves by position instead, so they stay clean.
  • Visible 2px accent :focus-visible outlines at 2px offset, never removed without replacement; skip-link at the top of the z-scale; the mobile nav toggle keeps a full 44px hit area; color-scheme: dark declared for native form rendering.
